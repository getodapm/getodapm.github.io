Why this exists

Restoration work is priced against a single proprietary list. Insurers use that same list — then discount the contractor's submission below it. The contractor has no independent reference to push back with. That isn't a market; it's an administered price, set by a party with an interest in keeping it low.

The fix isn't a contractor-friendly list — a list that tilts the other way is just as broken. The fix is a transparent benchmark no party controls, where every number traces to a public, checkable source.

And the old moat is gone. The proprietary list's power was never the prices — it was the cost of collecting the data. In the AI era, public wage data, material cost indices, and equipment rates can reconstruct defensible market pricing for almost nothing, and show the work. Their data no longer matters.

The principles

1 · Market-derived, not hand-set

Every price comes from citable open inputs. The methodology is public. Anyone can audit why a number is what it is.

2 · Capture all defensible work

Every line ties to a justification — the standard, the observed condition, the documentation. Completeness survives scrutiny.

3 · One honest price

The same transparent numbers whether insurance pays in full, in part, or the homeowner self-pays. No two-tier pricing.

4 · Homeowners are people, not claims

Damage happens to responsible people; help them. Deferred damage genuinely costs more — that's physics, not a judgment.

What ODAPM is

One open standard, two layers, and a separate tax sibling:

Scope schema — a vendor-neutral language for restoration line items: what work exists, its units, its action choices, how an estimate is structured. Any app can build on it.
Pricing layer — flat, auditable rates attached to each item, derived once from a cost model (labor hours × local wage + materials + markup), anchored to the owner's accepted invoices, reconciled through tolerance bands, and escalated by public indices.
Tax sibling — jurisdiction rates from each state's own published data, mapped to the territory you actually serve. Tax applies to materials only; labor is never taxed.

Build your model — no coding

Get the repository (clone it, or download the ZIP from GitHub).
Open the folder in an AI assistant that can read and write files (Claude).
Open prompts/00-START-HERE.md and run the prompts in order — your trade, your territory, your scope, your prices.
Walk away with model.json and tax.json: your complete, defensible pricing model. Yours. Free.

The reference implementation — a free, self-contained estimating app that consumes ODAPM models — lives at DRYscope.

Owned by no one

ODAPM is free and open source and will stay that way. It contains zero proprietary or third-party pricing data — every number derives from cited open sources. The work it prices is where contractors earn. The standard itself belongs to the field.

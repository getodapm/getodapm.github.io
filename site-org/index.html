OpenData an AI Pricing Model

Trade work is sold as SKUs. Fair price is public market data plus the markup you disclose, with a basis that shows the work.

Why this exists

If you were a plumber, you’d want standard SKUs for the labor — installing a water heater. Handyman, HVAC, electrical, a GC’s unit prices: same idea. Trade work is sold as SKUs. Most of those prices are a guess, a copied competitor, or a black-box list.

The fix is a priced catalog you can audit: public data sets the cost floor, you set markup and disclose it, every SKU carries a basis that shows the work.

AI is how you build that catalog for your trade and territory. That is the product of the standard.

The principles

01

Market-derived, not hand‑set

The cost floor comes from citable open inputs: wages, materials, equipment when they apply. The methodology is public. Anyone can audit why a number is what it is.

02

Show your work

Every non-zero price carries a basis. Sources, hours, markup — visible. A number without a basis is not a price.

03

Standard SKUs for standard work

Name the work, unitize it, reuse it. A water-heater install, hang a door, a framed wall, a dried room. Same work, same SKU.

04

You set markup; public data sets the cost floor

The market inputs are shared. Your margin is yours, and it is disclosed. Hide neither the cost nor the markup.

What OpenData is

One open standard for trade SKUs. Two layers, and a separate tax sibling. The brand is OpenData; the spec line is still odapm/v1.

Scope

A vendor-neutral language for the work you sell: what SKUs exist, their units, their options, how a job is structured. Any app can build on it.

Pricing

Auditable unit prices on each SKU. Derived from a public cost model (labor hours × local wage + materials + equipment share + disclosed markup), each non-zero price carrying a basis note, then escalated by public indices.

Tax

Jurisdiction rates from each state’s published data. Tax applies to materials only; labor is never taxed.

Build your model — no coding

Get the repository (clone it, or download the ZIP from GitHub).
Open the folder in an AI assistant that can read and write files.
Open prompts/00-START-HERE.md and run the prompts in order — your trade, your territory, your SKUs, your prices.
Walk away with model.json and tax.json: a priced catalog you can audit. Yours.

Owned by no one

The OpenData standard is free and open and owned by no one. Tools are MIT; spec and data are CC-BY-4.0. Zero proprietary pricing data — every number derives from cited open sources. Shops earn on the work. The standard belongs to the field.
